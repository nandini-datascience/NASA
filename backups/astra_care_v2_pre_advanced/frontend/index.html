About ASTRA CARE Mission Architecture

Astronaut Health and Space Radiation Monitoring System built for deep-space mission safety, telemetry validation, and dosimetry intelligence.

Mission Overview

Deep-space exploration exposes crews to galactic cosmic rays (GCR) and solar particle events (SPE), alongside extreme microgravity physiological stresses. ASTRA CARE addresses this challenge through an integrated computational engine combining space radiation dosimetry and continuous astronaut vital sign surveillance.

Core Capabilities
  • Integration with NASA RadLab space radiation repository
  • Unit preservation: Absorbed dose rate (µGy/hr) and dose-equivalent rate (µSv/hr)
  • Validated trapezoidal numerical integration for cumulative space radiation
  • Automated telemetry timestamp gap detection and anomaly alerts
  • Physiological health telemetry with multi-parameter validation rules
  • Persistent SQLite database storage preventing duplicate measurements

Regulatory & Scientific Disclaimers
1. Scientific NASA RadLab Data Disclaimer

Space radiation measurements retrieved through the ASTRA CARE backend originate from public NASA RadLab research archives collected by real radiation sensors aboard the International Space Station (ISS). In compliance with scientific accuracy requirements, historical research measurements are strictly distinguished from real-time operational telemetry.

2. Non-Diagnostic Medical Disclaimer

ASTRA CARE is designed strictly for research, mission simulation, and telemetry monitoring demonstration purposes. It does not provide clinical diagnoses, medical advice, or treatment recommendations. All automated physiological notifications must be verified by licensed flight surgeons and medical personnel.

3. Data Classification Integrity

All physiological entries submitted via the Health Telemetry interface are transparently tagged with their origin: simulated_demo, real_sensor, or historical_research. Simulated demonstration records are never commingled or misrepresented as actual in-flight astronaut medical files.
